How AlignED works

AlignED helps you write a DepEd-aligned TLE or EPP lesson plan in your school's own format, from the official Budget of Work and Curriculum Guide, with Alan the owl beside you. Let Alan write the plan and check it, or write it yourself part by part while he guides you.

  • Your school's format. The plan comes out in the exact layout your school uses: DLP, ILAW, 4A's, PAL3, SNHS, TUP or one your teacher uploads.
  • Your curriculum. Standards and competencies are copied word for word from the Budget of Work or Curriculum Guide you pick, never made up.
  • Your choice of help. Building Proficiency writes the whole plan for you to review. Guided walks you through it so you write it yourself.

  1. Create your account

    On the sign-in page choose Sign up and use your school email (ending in @gsfe.tupcavite.edu.ph). AlignED emails you a 6-digit code; type it in to finish. Forgot your password later? Use Forgot Password? under the sign-in form.

  2. Join your teacher's class

    Open My Classes and enter the class code your teacher gives you. You need to be in a class before you can make lesson plans, and your teacher gives you the points Building Proficiency uses.

  3. Answer Alan's setup questions

    Alan asks nine short questions, one at a time: the format and school, how you will assess the lesson, who is teaching, the class, the subject and how long the discussion runs, the topic (and the topics before and after it), what he should read (a Budget of Work, Curriculum Guide or your own files), an optional sample plan, and how you want to write it.

  4. Write the plan your way

    Building Proficiency (30 points, once per plan): Alan writes the whole plan from your sources, and you read and edit it.

    Guided (free): you write each part yourself. Every part opens with a short explanation, and you can chat with Alan for ideas; for the lesson proper he writes out the whole lesson from your sources. If a part doesn't apply, choose None for this part. When every part is written, Alan organizes the plan's spacing, bullets and spelling, never what you wrote.

  5. Check it and save

    Open the document to see the plan in its real layout and edit anything directly. Check coherence tells you whether the objectives, lesson and evaluation fit together. Regenerate rewrites chosen parts (5 points). Then use Save lesson plan.

  6. Export and hand it in

    After saving, use Export as Word or Save as PDF. To hand it in, open List of Lesson Plans, open the plan's menu and choose Submit to class. You are notified when your teacher scores it, and the score and feedback show on the plan.

  7. Share it, if you like

    Make public (in the same menu) puts the plan in Public Lesson Plans, in the folder for its learning area. Others can read it but not download it, and teachers can rate it with stars.

Getting started

What does AlignED do?

It makes DepEd lesson plans for TLE and EPP in your school's own format. You answer a few setup questions, pick the Budget of Work or Curriculum Guide the lesson comes from, then either let Alan write the plan or write it yourself part by part with his help. The finished plan looks like your format and exports as a Word file or a PDF.

Who is Alan?

Alan is the owl who helps you through AlignED. He asks the setup questions, writes the plan in Building Proficiency, explains each part and answers your questions in Guided mode, and checks whether your plan hangs together.

What can students, teachers and admins each do?

Students join their teacher's class, make lesson plans and submit them. Teachers create sections, give students points, add their school's formats, make their own plans, and review and score what students submit. Admins manage accounts, sections, documents, the built-in formats and announcements for the whole school.

How do I get an account?

Students sign up themselves with their school email (ending in @gsfe.tupcavite.edu.ph) and the 6-digit code AlignED emails them. Teacher and admin accounts are made by your school's AlignED admin.

Can I see Alan's tour again?

Yes. Use Replay the tour at the top of this page. Alan opens each tab and points out what it is for.

Formats

What is a format?

It is the layout your plan is printed in, such as the DLP, ILAW, 4A's, PAL3, SNHS DLP or TUP Semi-Detailed Lesson Plan. Every plan follows its format exactly, from the header table to the procedure.

Which formats can I use?

Teachers can use every ready-made format and any format they upload. Students can use the ready-made formats and every format their own teacher uploaded.

Why can't I edit the ready-to-use formats?

They are set up for everyone, so only the admin changes them. Teachers who need a different layout can upload their own in Formats with New format.

How do I add my school's own format?

Teachers: open Formats, choose New format and upload a blank copy as a .docx, .dotx or .pdf. AlignED shows a live copy you can tidy up (fix typos, delete leftover lines) and finds the fillable fields. Check them before saving. Students can't upload formats; ask your teacher.

Formats call the same part by different names. Does AlignED know they are the same?

Yes. It recognizes the usual names, so "Lesson Proper", "Discussion", "Abstraction" and "Learning Experience" are all treated as the part that teaches the topic. For a name it doesn't know, it reads the field's What goes here note, so a part called "Self-Check" described as "a short quiz" is written as an evaluation.

What is "What goes here" on a field?

A sentence the format's owner writes to say what belongs in that part, for example "Three short classroom rules, one per line." Alan follows it when he writes the part and when he helps you in Guided mode, and it is shown to you while you write.

What does Locked mean on a field?

Alan never writes a locked field. You fill it in yourself, and AlignED only tidies its layout.

AlignED says it couldn't read my file. What now?

That usually means the PDF is a scan (a picture of a page) rather than real text. Upload a .docx copy of the format, or export the PDF again from Word.

Making a plan

Students: why can't I start a lesson plan?

You need to be in your teacher's class first. Open My Classes and enter the class code your teacher gave you.

What does Alan ask in the setup?

Nine questions, one at a time: the format and school, how you will assess the lesson, who is teaching, the class, the subject and the length of the discussion, the topic (with the topics before and after it), what he should read, an optional sample plan, and how you want to write it. You can go back and change any answer.

How do I choose the assessment?

Pick Based on my objectives (recommended) and Alan matches the assessment to what each objective asks. Or choose a multiple-choice quiz of 5, 10, 15 or 20 items with an answer key, a performance task with a rubric table, or both.

What should I pick for "What should I read?"

Pick the Budget of Work and/or Curriculum Guide for your subject and grade from the library, and add your own files if you have them. The standards and competencies are copied word for word from these, and the lesson is written from them. Files you upload here stay private to you.

What is the sample lesson plan for?

It is optional. A finished plan on any topic shows Alan what goes in each part and how much to write. He follows its style, never its topic.

Building Proficiency or Guided mode: which should I use?

Building Proficiency writes the whole plan at once, and you review and edit it after. Guided takes you through the parts one at a time: you write each one yourself, and Alan explains the part and helps in the chat. Choose Guided when you want to practise writing the plan yourself.

In Guided mode, will Alan write a part for me?

No. Guided is for writing it yourself. Alan explains what the part needs, answers your questions and gives ideas in the chat, but you write the part.

A part doesn't apply to my lesson. What do I do?

In Guided mode, choose None for this part. The part is marked "None" and you move on.

What happens when I finish all the parts in Guided mode?

Alan shows Organizing your lesson plan while he tidies it: the spacing between lines and parts, bullets and numbering, quiz items and tables, and spelling, grammar and punctuation. Your content stays as you wrote it. Then you see the review screen, where you can change any part, check coherence and open the document.

What does Check coherence do?

It reads the whole plan and tells you whether the objectives, the lesson and the evaluation fit together, and what to change if they don't. It is free.

What do points pay for?

Your teacher gives you points in My Sections. Having Alan write a whole plan in Building Proficiency uses 30 points, once per plan. Rewriting chosen parts later with Regenerate uses 5 points each time, and you get them back if it fails. Guided mode, Alan's chat and Check coherence are free. Teachers never use points.

Where do the references come from?

From the sources you picked, plus reliable web pages about your topic that Alan finds and checks open. Each reference is written in APA style, and the lesson proper is written from those pages.

Why is Session No. left blank?

Only your schedule knows which session of the term this is, so you fill it in yourself.

ILAW has a column for each day. How is that filled?

Each of those rows gets one answer per day, Monday to Friday, each in its own cell, so the week builds day by day.

The document looks small. Can I make it bigger?

The page is shown at its real size, fitted to your screen (Fit). Use the zoom buttons in the toolbar to make it bigger, and the zoom readout to go back to Fit.

A field came back empty after generating. What do I do?

Write it in yourself, ask Alan about it in the chat, or rewrite it with Regenerate. A standard or competency stays empty when your Budget of Work or Curriculum Guide doesn't have it; pick a guide that covers your lesson.

Course guides and subjects

Which subjects does AlignED support?

ICT, Computer Programming (CP), AFA (Agri-Fishery Arts), HE (Home Economics, now called Family and Consumer Science or FCS), IA (Industrial Arts), and Electrical (Electrical and Electronics Servicing).

Where do the official guides come from?

From DepEd: the MATATAG EPP/TLE Curriculum Guide and the Three-Term Budgets of Work published on the DepEd LR Portal, plus the TESDA-based K to 12 guides. They are marked as official and can't be changed.

Can I add my own course guide?

Teachers can: open Curriculum/Course Guide and use Upload a guide, and everyone can then find it in the library. Students can't upload to the library, but can add their own files in the setup's "What should I read?" step; those stay private.

Saving, drafts and exporting

Is my work saved as I go?

Yes, drafts save automatically while you work. If AlignED says it couldn't save, use Save lesson plan. It also warns you before you leave a page with unsaved work.

Where are the plans I haven't finished?

In List of Lesson Plans, under My Lesson Plans. Open a draft's menu and choose Continue to pick up where you left off.

How do I get a Word copy?

Save the plan first, then use Export as Word. Saved plans can also be downloaded from their menu in List of Lesson Plans. The file keeps your format's exact layout and font.

How do I get a PDF?

Use Save as PDF. Your browser's print window opens; choose "Save as PDF" as the printer.

Sharing and public plans

How do I share a lesson plan?

In List of Lesson Plans, open the plan's menu and choose Make public. Choose Make private to stop sharing it.

How are public plans organized?

Public Lesson Plans has a folder for each learning area: ICT, CP, HE, AGRI, IA and Electrical. A plan goes in the folder for the subject chosen in its setup. Type in the search box to look through every folder at once.

Can I download someone else's public plan?

No. Public plans can be previewed but not downloaded. The author downloads their own plan from My Lesson Plans.

What are the star ratings?

Teachers can rate a student's plan from 1 to 5 stars once it is public or submitted to their class. Each teacher has one rating per plan and can change it. The average shows next to the plan's title, and the student is notified when a teacher rates it.

Sections and submissions

Teachers: how do students join my class?

Go to My Sections and use Create Section. Each section gets a join code; give it to your students. You can make a new code if the old one gets out.

Teachers: how do I give students points?

In My Sections, open the section and give points to the students who need them.

Students: how do I join a class and submit a plan?

Open My Classes and enter the code your teacher gave you. Then, in List of Lesson Plans, open the plan's menu and choose Submit to class.

Teachers: where do I review what students submit?

In List of Lesson Plans, on the Student Submissions tab. Pick a section, then Open & review. Give a score from 1 to 100 and feedback, and use Mark as reviewed. Each submission can also be downloaded as Word or saved as a PDF.

Students: how do I see my score?

You are notified when your teacher reviews your plan. The score and feedback show on the plan in List of Lesson Plans and in its preview.

Announcements and the tutorial video

Where do I find announcements?

New ones show at the top of your pages and in your notifications. All of them are in Announcements.

Where is the tutorial video?

When your admin posts one, it plays inside its announcement. Open Announcements, or use Watch the tutorial at the top of this page.

Admins: how do I post a video?

In Announcements, create or edit an announcement and choose a video (MP4 or WebM, up to 500 MB). It uploads in small pieces with a progress bar, so keep the window open until it finishes.

Your account

How do I change my name, email or password?

Open Settings, from the menu or your picture in the top corner.

I forgot my password.

On the sign-in page, choose Forgot Password?. AlignED emails you a code to set a new one.

I can't sign in. What should I do?

Check your email and password, or reset the password. If it still fails, your account may have been archived; ask your school admin.
